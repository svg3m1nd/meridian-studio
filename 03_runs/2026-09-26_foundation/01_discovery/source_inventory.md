## Evidence labels

- **Direct:** Explicitly supplied or confirmed by the product owner.
- **Source claim:** Asserted by the supplied presentation or prototype; may still require primary-source verification.
- **Inference:** Product implication derived from supplied evidence.
- **Open:** Materially unresolved.

## Presentation-derived conceptual model

The deck names eight **ranking-influence layers**:

| Layer | Claimed function | Meridian implication |
|---|---|---|
| Link | Authority | Observe page/link relationships and source proximity without reducing authority to link counts |
| Entity / Knowledge | Understanding | Resolve canonical entities, aliases, and typed relationships |
| Topical salience | Depth | Measure how central entities and concepts are within documents and collections |
| Behavioral | Satisfaction | Store observable engagement signals with source, window, and collection limitations |
| Authorship / Identity | Provenance | Connect people, credentials, publications, and content while preserving identity uncertainty |
| Local / Review | Prominence | Model businesses, places, listings, reviews, mentions, recency, and consistency |
| Trust / Spam | Safety and trust | Evaluate source and neighborhood trust separately from popularity |
| Citation | Scholarly or evidentiary echo | Track formal citations and answer-engine citations as typed edges with context |

## Critical modeling boundary

The eight presentation layers are not automatically eight database graphs. They are an analyst-facing explanatory framework for ranking influences. Meridian's canonical graph families are storage and reasoning structures. A single observation or edge may contribute evidence to multiple influence layers, and a layer may draw from several graph families.

## Source quality boundary

The deck deliberately mixes patents, sworn testimony, peer-reviewed research, official documentation, reporting, reverse engineering, leaks, and practitioner studies. Meridian must preserve those evidence classes rather than presenting them as equally authoritative.
